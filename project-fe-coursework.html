Overview

Everything from my graduate financial engineering coursework at USC in one place: five projects and a full set of course notes. The projects run across the derivatives landscape, from curve bootstrapping and lattice pricing through Monte Carlo exotics and GARCH volatility to credit models and a 30-year annuity guarantee; each notebook states its assumptions, derives its formulas, and keeps every result in executed cells. The table below links every notebook on GitHub, and the chapter guide at the bottom opens the notes PDF at any chapter.

Projects

# Project Description Notebook
1 Term Structure, Swaps, and Lattice Pricing Builds the SOFR discount curve under three interpolation schemes, prices bonds, swaps, and forward swaps off it, then values American, barrier, and dividend-adjusted options on binomial and trinomial trees; par bonds reprice to exactly 100.00 under all three curves. Open
2 Monte Carlo Option Pricing Prices exotics by simulation: Asian and knockout payoffs, correlated two-asset baskets, bump-and-revalue Greeks, and American exercise by least squares Monte Carlo; a weekly delta-hedging book on 100,000 sold calls replicates for 782,038 against 1,134,848 collected. Open
3 Credit Risk Models Covers the credit stack end to end: Merton's structural model calibrated to equity, hazard curves bootstrapped from CDS quotes, rating-transition bond valuation under a financial crisis stress, and one-factor Gaussian pricing of synthetic tranches with Vasicek loss distributions; the Merton calibration backs out a 0.477 percent market spread. Open
4 Volatility, Market Risk, and Real Options Estimates GARCH(1,1) by maximum likelihood with normal and Student-t errors, simulates portfolio VaR and expected shortfall under Vasicek rates, compresses the Treasury curve with PCA, and prices abandonment and expansion real options on a futures-calibrated tree; volatility persistence lands at 0.988. Open
5 Variable Annuity Guarantee Pricing Values a 30-year guaranteed withdrawal benefit from the insurer's side: seeded Monte Carlo with equity returns correlated to stochastic Vasicek rates, annual mortality, and fee income netted against shortfalls, under both risk-neutral and real-world measures; starting rates at 1 percent instead of 5 flip the value from 4.6091 to negative 14.7196 per 100 of premium. Open
